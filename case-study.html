Clear to Register

A student-portal feature that shows undergraduates every hold and financial task blocking registration, who owns it, what to do next, and where it stands. I took it from research to a release plan.

1 · Problem

Students find out about holds when it is too late to fix them

Registration holds and financial tasks are spread across the bursar, financial aid, advising and the registrar. Each office uses its own wording and its own system. Students often learn about a hold close to registration day, sometimes only when the register button fails.

I started with a hypothesis: if students could see every blocker in one place, with the owning office and a clear next step, more of them would be ready before their registration window opened, and offices would get fewer “why can’t I register?” calls.

2 · Research

The problem was ownership and next steps, not just visibility

I wrote five assumptions, ranked them by risk, and tested them in interviews with four undergraduates and one student-services staff member.

  • 2 of 4 students found out about their hold late, close to or on registration day.
  • Students could often see that a hold existed. They didn’t know which office owned it or what to do next.
  • After submitting something, students didn’t know if it had worked. Several called the office to check.
  • The staff member confirmed that many calls before registration are status questions.

Assumption scorecard

AssumptionResult
A1–A3: students miss holds, don’t know the owner, don’t know the next stepPartly supported
A4–A5: students would act on reminders; offices would see fewer callsNot yet confirmed

A4 and A5 became success metrics to measure in the pilot instead of facts to build on.

3 · Requirements

A PRD built around one question: am I clear to register?

The PRD sets out 10 functional requirements. These include a readiness status on Home, a list of every blocker, plain-language explanations with numbered next steps, office contacts, “last updated” times, an “Under review” state and reminders 30, 14 and 7 days out. It also covers edge cases such as unknown hold types, missing registration dates and data that is late to update.

Because the feature shows student financial and academic records, the non-functional requirements cover FERPA, the GLBA Safeguards Rule, SSO-only access, a 15-minute session timeout, privacy-safe reminders that never include balances, and WCAG 2.1 AA.

4 · Design

Four screens from “something’s wrong” to “here’s how to fix it”

The main flow follows Maya, a student with two blocking items, from Home to the item list, the item detail and reminder settings. Every item shows its owner, due date and status, and every detail page ends with the office’s contact details.

5 · Usability testing

Three tests, three fixes

I ran the clickable prototype with three students. Everyone found their blockers and the next step without help. The status tags and the “Need help?” card worked as intended. Three moments caused confusion, and each one led to a design change and a new acceptance criterion in Jira.

After the portal · KAN-8

“Do I need to come back and mark this done?”

Students weren’t sure what happened after they uploaded the document.

Fix: a note under the portal button. The item changes to “Under review” automatically within 4 hours, review takes up to 3 business days, and nothing needs to be marked done.

Under review · KAN-12

“Under review” read as “done”

With one item under review, students assumed they could register, even though a balance was still blocking them.

Fix: a summary banner (“Not clear to register yet · 1 of 2 items under review · 1 still blocking”), a clock icon on the tag and a plain explanation of what review means.

Reminders · KAN-14

The preview looked like a real alert

The sample notification on the settings screen was read as the student’s actual status.

Fix: removed the on-screen preview and added a “Send test reminder” button. Students now see a real sample on their own phone or email, and it never shows their actual items.

Deferred: custom reminder dates and add-to-calendar. One tester asked for them, but they weren’t needed to get students clear. They are tracked as KAN-16.

6 · Backlog

An epic, 11 stories and a spike, ready for two sprints

Each story has a user-story statement, Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, an estimate and a link back to its PRD requirement and design screen. Sprint 1 starts with a spike to confirm how hold data can be read from the student information system, because every other story depends on it.

Sprint 1 · the core experience

  • Spike: confirm how to read hold data
  • Readiness status on Home
  • List of every hold and financial task
  • Plain-language explanations and next steps
  • Privacy and security review (FERPA, GLBA)

Sprint 2 · trust and follow-through

  • “Last updated” time and pending-update state
  • Owning office’s contact details
  • “Under review” state and readiness banner
  • Reminders and reminder channel choices
  • WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility
7 · UAT and release

Tested with six realistic test accounts, released in three gated phases

The UAT plan has 23 test cases mapped to the Jira stories. It uses six test accounts, each set up for a hard case: two holds, no holds, an item under review, a pending data update, no registration date and an unknown hold type. The release is behind a feature flag, so it can be turned off without a redeploy.

Phase 1

Internal test

UAT with staff from the registrar, financial aid and student accounts offices.

Gate: all high-priority UAT cases pass; privacy review signed off.

Phase 2

Pilot

Transfer students, who are more likely to have holds and less familiar with the offices.

Gate: +5 pts on the north star vs. non-pilot students; reminder opt-outs under 10%.

Phase 3

Full rollout

All undergraduates, timed ahead of the next registration window.

Rollback: turn off the feature flag if data errors or a spike in support contacts appear.

What I learned

Testing changed what “done” meant

The biggest design risk wasn’t finding the hold. It was the time between taking action and the system catching up. All three usability fixes were about that gap. They became acceptance criteria and UAT cases, so the build has to match what students actually needed.

Next, I would get baseline numbers from registrar and help-desk data before the pilot. I would also test whether reminders actually change behavior, since that assumption is still unconfirmed.

The PRD, problem brief, UAT plan and Jira backlog live in private workspaces and are available on request. All screens use a fictional student, and the office contact details are placeholders.
